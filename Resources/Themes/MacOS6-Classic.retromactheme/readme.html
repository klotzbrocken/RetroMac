Macintosh System 6

Apple · 1988

System 6 (1988) is the classic 1-bit black-and-white Macintosh: crisp Chicago type, the Finder, pull-down menus and the desktop metaphor Apple introduced in 1984. On a compact Mac's 512×342 screen there were no greys — just black, white and clever dithering.

MultiFinder let you keep several programs open at once (cooperatively), and the whole system booted from an 800 KB floppy.

What made it special

  • The 1-bit black-and-white desktop, dithered patterns and Chicago font.
  • MultiFinder — optional cooperative multitasking.
  • The Finder, trash can and drag-and-drop that defined the desktop metaphor.
  • Small enough to boot from a floppy.

ⓘ  Did you know…

Happy Mac, Sad Mac. A smiling Mac icon meant a healthy boot; a sad Mac (with cryptic error codes) meant trouble — often accompanied by the “Chimes of Death”.
It fit on a floppy. The entire System 6 could run from a single 800 KB disk — smaller than a few of today's photos.
Chicago everywhere. The bold Chicago typeface from the menus later showed up on the original iPod's screen.
