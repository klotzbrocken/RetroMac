KDE 1.0

The K Desktop Environment · 12 July 1998

In October 1996 Matthias Ettrich, a student in Tübingen, asked on Usenet for help with a "Kool Desktop Environment": one consistent desktop for Unix, where every program looked alike and could be configured without editing text files. Twenty months later, on 12 July 1998, KDE 1.0 came out.

It brought the panel along the bottom with the K menu, launchers, four virtual desktops and the clock, a taskbar along the top with a button for every window, the kwm window manager with its shaded blue title bars, and the KFM file manager that also drew the desktop icons.

In RetroMac

  • The K menu sorts your Mac apps into KDE's groups by the category each app declares. "All Applications" lists every one, and "Run Command..." starts one by typing its name.
  • The panel buttons open the nearest Mac app: KVT is Terminal, KEdit TextEdit, KNotes Stickies, KMail your mail app, the KDE Control Center System Settings.
  • One to Four switch between the four backgrounds KDE 1.0 shipped with. Your Mac windows stay where they are: macOS cannot move a single window to another desktop without minimising it.
  • Logout ends the KDE session, which switches the theme off. Your Mac session stays.

Did you know?

KDE was built on Trolltech's Qt, which was not free software in 1998. The dispute led to the GNOME project and was settled when Qt went GPL in 2000.

The K button's tooltip asked "Where do you want to go tomorrow?", a dig at Microsoft's slogan of the day.

Sizes, colours and the four backgrounds come from KDE's own sources and configuration (kde1-kdebase, 10 July 1998). The icons are KDE 1.0's originals; the pictures for today's apps were drawn for RetroMac in the same style.
