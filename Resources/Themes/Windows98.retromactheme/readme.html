Windows 98

Microsoft · 1998

Windows 98 polished the grey-and-teal Windows 95 formula and wired the web into the desktop. It brought the Quick Launch bar, Windows Update, Active Desktop, proper USB support and the FAT32 file system, all under those blue-gradient title bars and the Tahoma font.

It sat on the consumer 9x/DOS lineage, and for a lot of households it was the machine that first got online.

What made it special

  • The Quick Launch bar and Windows Update.
  • USB plug-and-play that finally worked in daily use.
  • Active Desktop put live web content on your wallpaper.
  • FAT32, larger disks and the familiar blue title bars.

ⓘ  Did you know…

The blue screen heard round the world. During a live 1998 demo of USB plug-and-play, a test PC crashed to a Blue Screen of Death on stage next to Bill Gates. “That must be why we're not shipping Windows 98 yet,” quipped his presenter to a roar of laughter.
The web on your desktop. Active Desktop could pin web pages and channels straight onto the wallpaper — an early, ambitious mash-up of browser and desktop.
USB grows up. 98 was where USB devices became genuinely plug-and-play for most people.
