OS/2 Warp 4

IBM · 1996 (codename “Merlin”)

OS/2 began in 1987 as a joint IBM–Microsoft project to succeed DOS — until Microsoft peeled off to pour its energy into Windows. IBM carried on alone, and Warp 4 (1996, codenamed “Merlin”) was the high point: the object-oriented Workplace Shell, the WarpCenter, built-in Java, and even speech recognition out of the box.

It could run DOS and Windows 3.x programs, and its rock-solid multitasking kept it running ATMs and back-office banking systems long after the desktop war was lost.

What made it special

  • The Workplace Shell — a genuinely object-oriented desktop.
  • Ran DOS and Windows 3.x apps alongside native OS/2 ones.
  • VoiceType speech recognition, built in — in 1996.
  • Pre-emptive multitasking that just would not fall over.

ⓘ  Did you know…

Talk to your computer, in 1996. Warp 4 shipped with VoiceType, letting you dictate and control the system by voice — years before it was common anywhere else.
A partnership that reshaped the industry. OS/2 was born from IBM and Microsoft working together; when they split, Microsoft took the ideas into Windows NT.
Quietly everywhere. Long after 1996, OS/2 kept humming inside ATMs, ticket machines and bank branches thanks to its reliability.
