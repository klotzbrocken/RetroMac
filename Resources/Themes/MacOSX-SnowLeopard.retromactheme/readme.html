Snow Leopard

Apple · Mac OS X 10.6, 2009

Snow Leopard (10.6, 2009) is the release Apple famously sold on having no new features. Instead it was a deep clean: a smaller, faster, fully 64-bit system that dropped support for the old PowerPC chips and reclaimed gigabytes of disk space.

Its refinements went under the hood — Grand Central Dispatch for multicore, OpenCL for the GPU — and it earned a lasting reputation as one of the most stable Macs ever.

What made it special

  • Sold on polish and speed, not features.
  • Grand Central Dispatch made multicore programming practical.
  • OpenCL put the graphics card to work on general computing.
  • Fully 64-bit; freed roughly 7 GB of disk.

ⓘ  Did you know…

Zero new features. Apple's own marketing leaned into it — the headline was refinement, reliability and a smaller footprint rather than a feature list.
The last of Rosetta. Snow Leopard was the final OS X that could run old PowerPC apps via Rosetta; 10.7 dropped it.
Still beloved. Years later many users called it the high-water mark for Mac stability.
