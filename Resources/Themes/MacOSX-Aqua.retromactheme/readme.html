OS X Mountain Lion

Apple · OS X 10.8, 2012

OS X Mountain Lion (10.8, 2012) pulled the Mac and the iPhone closer together. It brought Notification Center, Messages (iMessage), Notes, Reminders, Game Center and tight iCloud sync to the desktop, and quietly dropped the word “Mac” from the name.

It was the high point of Apple's skeuomorphic era — leather-bound Calendar, faux-linen backgrounds — and set the once-a-year OS release rhythm that continues today.

What made it special

  • Notification Center and iMessage came to the Mac.
  • Gatekeeper added a layer of download security.
  • AirPlay mirroring to an Apple TV.
  • The textured, skeuomorphic look, just before the flat redesign of Yosemite.

ⓘ  Did you know…

iPhone, meet Mac. Mountain Lion folded a wave of iOS apps and ideas into OS X — Messages, Reminders, Notes and Notification Center all crossed over.
Goodbye “Mac”. Apple began calling it simply “OS X” here, and moved to a yearly release cadence.
Peak skeuomorphism. Stitched leather and torn-paper apps were everywhere — the very look Jony Ive's flat redesign would sweep away two years later.
