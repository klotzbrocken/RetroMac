Amiga Workbench

Commodore / Hyperion · Workbench debuted 1985, 4.1 in 2008

The Amiga arrived in 1985 with something no home computer had: real pre-emptive multitasking, driven by custom chips (Agnus, Denise and Paula) that handled graphics and four-channel sound in hardware. Workbench was its desktop — folders as “drawers”, disks on the workbench, a spare grey-blue-orange palette.

Workbench 4.1 (2008, Hyperion) is the modern PowerPC-era AmigaOS, keeping the drawers-and-Workbench metaphor decades after Commodore itself was gone.

What made it special

  • Pre-emptive multitasking in 1985 — years before the Mac or Windows managed it.
  • Custom silicon for graphics, blitter and sound made the Amiga a video and demo-scene legend.
  • Powered the Video Toaster and the CGI for TV shows like Babylon 5.
  • The Guru Meditation — the Amiga's cryptic red crash screen.

ⓘ  Did you know…

The Boing Ball. At CES 1984 a bouncing, spinning checkered ball with a stereo thud stunned onlookers — a demo showing the custom chips off before the Amiga even shipped. It became the machine's mascot.
Ahead of its time. True multitasking, a 4096-colour mode and hardware sprites in a 1985 home computer that cost a fraction of a workstation.
Hollywood & TV. Amigas running the Video Toaster produced broadcast graphics and effects on a budget for years.
